Hi, I’m Rick. I’m a planetary scientist at the Space Telescope Science Institute in Baltimore, where I study how energy and momentum move through the atmospheres of the giant planets. I also work as an instrument scientist on the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, leading the Pipeline and Archive block of the Roman Telescope Branch and developing the Wide Field Instrument Reference File Pipeline.

My research connects the deep atmospheres of Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune (where convection, waves and jet streams are born, hidden beneath thick clouds) to the upper atmospheres we can observe directly. I combine general circulation models, turbulence theory and multi-wavelength observations from ALMA, the VLA, Hubble and the IRTF to measure winds, temperatures and turbulence, and to work out what drives phenomena like Jupiter’s quasi-quadrennial oscillation. I’m particularly interested in using large astrophysical observatories, and soon Roman, for Solar System science, and in carrying these comparative-planetology lessons to exoplanets and the Habitable Worlds Observatory.

Before astronomy, I spent five years as an electrical engineer, and I still love the place where instrumentation, software and science meet. I earned my PhD in Physics from New Mexico Tech in Socorro, a short drive from the Very Large Array, where I was an NRAO Grote Reber Doctoral Fellow. I then held a NASA Postdoctoral Program fellowship at Goddard Space Flight Center, where I also worked as an instrument scientist on OSIRIS-REx’s OVIRS spectrometer.
