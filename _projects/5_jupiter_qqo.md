Jupiter's equatorial stratosphere oscillates with a roughly four-year period, the quasi-quadrennial oscillation (QQO), much like Earth's Quasi-Biennial Oscillation. Using nearly five years of TEXES/IRTF temperature maps spanning a full cycle, together with general circulation modeling, I showed that waves generated by convection in the weather layer deposit momentum aloft, producing jet reversals and downward-propagating shear zones.

{% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/proj_qqo.jpg" title="Stratospheric temperature oscillation (illustration)" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}

This work ([Cosentino et al. 2017, JGR Planets](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JE005342)) was featured in a [NASA press release](/press/) and a documentary on Jupiter. Follow-up studies examined the meridional thermal structure of the stratosphere ([Cosentino et al. 2020, PSJ](https://doi.org/10.3847/psj/abbda3)) and fluctuations in the oscillation ([Antuñano et al. 2021, Nature Astronomy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-020-1165-5)).
