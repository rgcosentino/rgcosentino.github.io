Is Jupiter's weather layer thin or deep? Turbulence theory offers a test: two-dimensional turbulence moves energy from small storms up to large structures like the Great Red Spot, while three-dimensional turbulence cascades energy downward to small scales.

With **VLA** observations probing below the visible clouds (program 15A-320), I found a 2D turbulence signature indicating a shallow weather layer ([Cosentino et al. 2017, Icarus](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2017.01.006)). Extending the method to every available **Hubble** map of Jupiter, I showed that the bright zones and dark belts behave differently: zones are organized at the Rhines scale of the jets, while belts are powered by convective storms ([Cosentino et al. 2019, JGR Planets](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018JE005762)). The power spectra are available as a [high-level science product at MAST](https://archive.stsci.edu/prepds/jtps/). Next: applying the technique to Saturn with Hubble OPAL data.
